01 · Limits

Every plan window, one glance up.

Collapsed, the notch shows a ring and a number for the account closest to trouble. Hover and it opens into session, weekly and billing meters, reset times and the session you are in right now. Model limits, Codex Spark, credits and extra usage share one line under the meters.

  • Every agent home is its own account: ~/.claude, ~/.claude_multi/*, ~/.codex*, ~/.gemini, Cursor and OpenCode Go are found on first launch
  • Copilot (github.com or GHE.com) and OpenRouter are added by hand

02 · Pace

Not just how much. How fast.

81% used sounds bad on a Thursday and fine on a Sunday. The white tick on each meter is where you would be at an even pace. Run ahead of it and the color moves from green to amber to red, with a forecast of when you run out.

  • On track within 5 points of the clock
  • Ahead of pace up to 12 points over
  • Runs out 7:24 PM when the trend beats the reset

03 · Accounts

Many homes. One clear picture.

Two Claude or Codex homes? When one reaches 85% of a window, the account page and the limit alert point to the one with the most room left, and copy the command that starts your agent there. Or show all of a provider's accounts on one page, each limit averaged across them.

  • CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/.claude_work claude, CODEX_HOME=~/.codex_work codex
  • Open it straight in Ghostty, iTerm2 or Terminal

04 · Alerts

Warned early. Never nagged.

An alert drops out of the notch at 75%, 90% and 100% of a window, with the projected run-out time. Burning fast warns sooner, when the pace would empty a window before it resets. Use it or lose it reminds you when a weekly window resets tomorrow with more than half left.

  • Each alert fires once per window, with optional sound
  • Full-screen apps and screen sharing hold alerts back

05 · Quiet

Heads down. The notch keeps quiet.

Set quiet hours for the evening, or tap the moon to snooze for an hour, until morning, or until you say so. Approvals and limit-reached alerts still get through, unless you silence those too.

  • Follows a macOS Focus: one Shortcuts automation runs agentilo quiet on
  • agentilo quiet 45m, off or status from any script

06 · Agents

All your agents, one pulse.

Running Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI sessions in any terminal, what each is doing, and which one is waiting for you. A dot on the pill pulses when one needs input. Click a row to bring its terminal forward.

  • Found from processes and transcripts with no setup; herdr and agent hooks add more detail
  • Hooks are marked and reversible; your other hooks stay untouched

07 · Replies

Finished? Read the gist first.

While an agent works, the pill spins with its turn time. When it finishes, the alert shows the start of its reply and how many tools it used, so you know whether the window needs you yet.

  • From the agents' own hooks, or the end of the transcript
  • The reply stays on your Mac; the local API never serves it

08 · Approvals

Say yes without switching windows.

Opt in and Claude Code permission prompts show up in the notch with the full command and Allow, Deny or Always. Leave one unanswered and it goes back to the terminal after a timeout, as does any prompt for the pane you are already looking at.

09 · Activity

Half a year of work, in one glance.

A 26-week heatmap and streak, today's prompts, tool calls and sessions, top projects, and what it all would cost at API list prices. Daily totals are kept on disk, so history outlives the agents' own 30-day log cleanup.

10 · Reports

Where the week went.

This week against the last: cost at API list prices, tokens, prompts, and the projects and models behind them. On Monday morning a summary drops in. The Reports window charts 7 days to 26 weeks by project, model or agent.

  • Claude at Anthropic's list prices, Codex at OpenAI's
  • Split by project and model, kept beyond the agents' 30-day log cleanup

Security and privacy

Your tokens stay home.

No account, no backend, no analytics. Agentilo is a local app that talks only to the providers you already use.

Read-only credentials

Uses the sign-ins your tools already store. Never refreshes or rewrites them, and sends each only to its own provider. Nothing it calls spends a limit reset or changes your account.

No telemetry

The only network calls are the providers' usage endpoints, through your proxy if you set one, and a daily update check against this project's releases.

Loopback-only API

Listens on 127.0.0.1, refuses browser pages, and needs a per-install token for anything that changes state. Account labels are left out unless you allow them.

Signed updates

Updates are verified with an EdDSA signature through Sparkle and replace the app in place. Every release is built in public CI from tagged source.

Proxy aware

System, HTTP, HTTPS or SOCKS5 proxy with optional authentication, per-provider overrides and a connection test. Passwords live in the Keychain.

Works offline

Offline or rate limited, Codex limits come from its own logs and Claude limits are estimated from tokens logged since the last check, clearly marked.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Does Agentilo need my passwords or an account?

No. It reads the sign-ins Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, OpenCode and the GitHub CLI already keep on your Mac. OpenRouter is the only provider that needs a key, which you paste into Settings and which stays in the Keychain.

Does it work without a notch?

Yes. On Macs and displays without a notch, Agentilo draws the same pill at the top center of the screen.

How accurate are the numbers?

Limits come straight from each provider's usage endpoint. When a provider is unreachable, Codex limits are read from its own logs and Claude limits are estimated from tokens logged since the last check; both are labeled. Costs are estimates at API list prices, since plans are not billed per token.

Is anything sent to a server you run?

No. There is no Agentilo server. The app talks to the providers' usage endpoints and checks this repository's releases once a day for updates.

Can I use it during a screen share or demo?

Yes. Choose to blur account names and emails, or to hide the notch entirely, while the screen is shared or recorded. Alerts then leave names out or wait until you are done.

Why is the app not notarized?

It is ad-hoc signed, so macOS asks once on first launch. Homebrew handles that for you; by hand, clear the quarantine flag once. Updates after that are verified with an EdDSA signature.
